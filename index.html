    The Invitation

    Every conversation about Africa is really many conversations. 54+ countries, a thousand languages, cities and villages, archives and futures. No single story has ever been big enough to hold the continent.

    The Single Story

    A single story travels fast and settles. It leaves everyone under-equipped: the reporter, the investor, the student, and the African whose own education skipped the continent's depth. Real conversations are lost before they begin.

    The Offer

    Reading Africa is a curated library. Countries come first, context travels with every entry, and depth is the point.

    Ground Rules for Thinking About Africa

    1

    Africa is not a country

    Conversations must be country-specific, region-specific, or issue-specific.

    2

    Development is not universal

    GDP growth and infrastructure mean different things in different contexts.

    3

    Progress is not linear

    Countries advance, stall, regress, and reconfigure over time.

    4

    Data without history is incomplete

    Numbers require political, economic, and cultural context.

    Professional Guide

    A Handbook for
    Better Reporting.

    Most journalists who misrepresent Africa don't mean to. They simply don't know better sources exist, and they work on tight deadlines. This handbook is here to help.

    Before You File

    01

    Check country-specific media outlets

    Don't rely solely on Western wire services.

    02

    Verify local data sources

    National statistics offices are often more accurate than global aggregates.

    03

    Include African voices

    Not as color, but as analysis. Researchers and experts should be primary sources.

    04

    Be time-specific

    'Africa's economy' means nothing without specifying which countries and which time period.

    05

    Understand political context

    Elections, coups, and policy changes. Look for the context that connects them.

    Red Flags

    Avoid these lazy tropes:

    • × "Africa is..." (unless talking about continental institutions)
    • × Only citing Western media or think tanks
    • × No African sources quoted directly
    • × Using one country's data to make claims about "Africa"
    • × Treating 54+ countries as a single economic/political unit
    • × Comparing African countries only to each other, not to similar economies globally
